# Journey Mapping Skill

This guide structures a customer journey map — the stages, actions, and emotional arc a customer moves through while trying to get a job done, including the parts outside the product.

**Core concept:** A journey map follows the customer's job, not the product's flow. It should start before the customer opens the product and end after they've gotten (or failed to get) the outcome they wanted, so it captures friction the product team doesn't normally see.

**Structure, one row per stage:**
- Stage — a distinct phase of the job (e.g. "realizes the problem," "evaluates options," "sets up," "gets value," "comes back")
- Actions — what the customer actually does at this stage
- Touchpoints — where the interaction happens (product, support, marketing, third party)
- Thoughts and emotions — quoted or paraphrased, not invented
- Pain points — specific friction, named concretely
- Opportunities — what could remove the friction or amplify the moment

**Common pitfalls:** Mapping the product's screens instead of the customer's experience. Building the map from internal assumptions instead of research (interviews, support tickets, session recordings, survey verbatims). Scoping too broadly to be actionable, or too narrowly to include the moments that actually cause drop-off. Treating the map as a one-time artifact instead of revisiting it as the product and market change.

**Deliverable:** A stage-by-stage table as above, plus a short list of the 2-3 highest-friction moments ranked by how many customers hit them and how severe the impact is — this is the prioritization input, not the map itself.
