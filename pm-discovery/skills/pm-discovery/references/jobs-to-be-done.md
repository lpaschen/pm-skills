# Jobs-to-be-Done Skill

This guide helps define what job a customer is hiring a product to do, so solutions get evaluated against the real progress a customer wants, not a feature wishlist.

**Core concept:** A job is the progress someone is trying to make in a given circumstance. People don't want a product, they want progress. The same job can be served by very different solutions, and the same product can serve very different jobs for different people.

**Job statement format:** "When [circumstance], I want to [motivation], so I can [expected outcome]." Anchor to the circumstance, not the customer's demographic profile — the same person hires different products in different situations.

**Three dimensions of a job:**
- Functional — the practical task to get done
- Emotional — how the customer wants to feel (or avoid feeling) while doing it
- Social — how the customer wants to be perceived by others

**Forces of progress:** push (dissatisfaction with the current approach), pull (appeal of the new approach), anxiety (concern about switching), habit (inertia of the status quo). A job worth pursuing has push and pull strong enough to outweigh anxiety and habit.

**Common pitfalls:** Defining the job as "use our product" instead of the progress underneath it. Scoping the job too broadly ("be productive") or too narrowly ("click this button"). Ignoring what the customer is currently hiring instead of your product, including non-software workarounds and doing nothing. Skipping the circumstance and describing a permanent trait instead of a situational trigger.

**Deliverable:** A job statement, the three-dimension breakdown, and a short list of what the customer currently "fires" (or would fire) to hire your product instead — this becomes the input for journey mapping, persona work, and positioning.
