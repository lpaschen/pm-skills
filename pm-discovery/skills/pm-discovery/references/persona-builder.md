# Persona Builder Skill

This guide builds a persona — a research-backed archetype of a user segment — so product decisions get evaluated against a specific set of goals and behaviors instead of an implicit "average user."

**Core concept:** A persona is a synthesis of patterns observed across real research (interviews, usage data, support tickets, surveys), not a demographic sketch invented in a workshop. If a detail in the persona can't be traced back to something a real user said or did, cut it.

**Structure:**
- Name and role — enough to make the persona memorable, not a full biography
- Primary job(s) to be done — what progress this person is trying to make
- Context — the circumstance they're usually in when the job shows up
- Behaviors — what they actually do today, including workarounds
- Goals and motivations — functional, emotional, social
- Frustrations — specific, evidenced friction, not generic complaints
- A representative quote — real or closely paraphrased from research
- Non-goals — who this persona explicitly is NOT, to keep it from drifting into "everyone"

**Common pitfalls:** Building from opinion or stakeholder assumption instead of research. Loading up on demographic detail (age, job title, hobbies) while leaving behavior and motivation thin — demographics rarely predict behavior. Maintaining too many personas, which dilutes focus instead of sharpening it. A persona that doesn't tie back to a specific job or a specific product decision is decoration, not a tool.

**Deliverable:** One persona per meaningfully distinct behavior pattern (usually 1-3 for a given product area), each traceable to the research that produced it.
