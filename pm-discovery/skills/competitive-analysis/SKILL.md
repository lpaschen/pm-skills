# Competitive Analysis

Research and analyze the competitive landscape. Identifies direct and indirect competitors,
maps positioning, builds feature comparisons, and surfaces differentiation opportunities
and strategic implications.

## Step 1: Scope the Analysis

Before diving in, ask:
- **What is your product?** What category does it compete in?
- **Which competitors?** Specific ones to analyze, or should I identify them?
- **What lens?** Feature comparison, positioning, pricing, go-to-market, or full landscape?
- **What's this for?** Product strategy, sales enablement, investor/board materials, or roadmap prioritization?

If the user has uploaded a competitor list, brief, or prior research — use it.

## Step 2: Identify the Competitive Set

Map competitors at multiple levels:

- **Direct**: Same problem, same buyer, same approach — products customers actively evaluate against you
- **Indirect**: Same problem, different approach (e.g., spreadsheets vs. dedicated tool)
- **Adjacent**: Don't compete today but could — larger platforms that could add your functionality, or startups in a nearby niche
- **Substitutes**: Entirely different ways users solve the underlying need (hiring a person, doing nothing, using a manual process)

Identify 5 direct competitors and 2–3 indirect or adjacent players. Note any emerging or disruptive entrants.

## Step 3: Research Each Competitor

**Web sources to use:**
- Product and pricing pages
- Recent blog posts, changelogs, and press releases
- G2, Capterra, and TrustRadius reviews (real user sentiment, unfiltered)
- Job postings (underrated signal — hiring ML engineers = AI investment)
- Funding announcements and press coverage
- Social media and community discussions

**If Slack is connected:** Search for competitive mentions in product or sales channels, recent deal feedback.

**If Google Drive or a knowledge base is connected:** Search for existing win/loss reports, prior competitive research, or battle cards.

For each competitor, gather:
- Company summary: founding, size, funding/revenue if public, target market
- Positioning: how they describe themselves, who they target, key messaging
- Recent momentum: launches, funding, partnerships, customer wins
- Pricing: model and price points (note if pricing requires sales contact)
- Strengths: what they genuinely do well, what customers praise
- Weaknesses: where they fall short, what customers complain about — be honest and evidence-based

## Step 4: Generate the Analysis

Use this structure, adapting sections to what's actually relevant:

```
## Competitive Analysis: [Your Product / Market]
**Date**: [today]
**Scope**: [what was analyzed]

### Market Overview
[2–3 sentences on market dynamics, key trends, and where the category is heading]

### Competitive Landscape

| Competitor | Type | Target | Positioning | Strength | Weakness |
|------------|------|--------|-------------|----------|----------|

### Feature Comparison

| Capability | Your Product | Competitor A | Competitor B | Competitor C |
|------------|-------------|-------------|-------------|-------------|

Use rating scale: **Strong** / **Adequate** / **Weak** / **Absent**
Rate based on real product experience and customer reviews — not marketing claims.
Weight by what matters to your target customers, not total feature count.

### Positioning Map
[2×2 matrix on the dimensions most relevant to your market — e.g., SMB vs Enterprise,
Self-serve vs Sales-led, Point solution vs Suite, Simple vs Powerful]

### Differentiation Opportunities
1. **[Opportunity]** — [why it's defensible and valuable, which competitors miss it]
2. ...

### Competitive Threats
1. **[Threat]** — [what to watch for, recommended response]
2. ...

### Strategic Implications
- **Double down on**: [your unique advantages worth investing in]
- **Close the gap on**: [table-stakes features you're missing — must-have to compete]
- **Ignore**: [competitor moves not worth responding to]
- **Monitor**: [trends or moves to watch quarterly]
```

## Step 5: Offer Next Steps

After delivering the analysis, offer:
- "Want me to build a **sales battle card** for a specific competitor?"
- "Should I draft a **'how to win against [competitor]'** guide?"
- "Want to develop **positioning or messaging** that differentiates from the top competitors?"
- "Should I identify **feature gaps to close** and suggest roadmap priorities?"
- "Want a **one-page executive summary** version?"

## Reference Frameworks

### Positioning Statement Analysis
For each competitor, extract their positioning using this template:

*For [target customer] who [need/problem], [Product] is a [category] that [key benefit]. Unlike [alternatives], [Product] [key differentiator].*

**Sources**: Homepage headline, app store descriptions, analyst briefing materials, earnings call language for public companies.

**Message architecture levels:**
- **Category**: What category do they claim? (CRM, project management, collaboration)
- **Differentiator**: What makes them different? (AI-powered, all-in-one, developer-first)
- **Value Proposition**: What outcome do they promise? (Close deals faster, ship faster)
- **Proof Points**: What evidence do they provide? (Customer logos, metrics, case studies)

**Look for**: Unclaimed positions no competitor owns, crowded claims that have lost meaning, vulnerable positions competitors can't fully deliver on.

### Win/Loss Analysis (if data is available)

The most actionable competitive intelligence. Ask:

**For wins**: What alternatives did you evaluate? Why did you choose us? What almost made you choose someone else?

**For losses**: What did you choose instead and why? Where did our product fall short? Would you reconsider us?

**Common patterns**: Feature gap, integration advantage, pricing model fit, incumbent advantage, sales execution, brand/trust.

### Market Trend Identification

For each significant trend: What is changing? Why now? Who is affected? What's the timeline? What are competitors doing about it?

**Strategic response options**: Lead (invest early), Fast follow (wait for demand signals then move), Monitor (set triggers), Ignore (explicitly decide it's not relevant — document why).

## Notes

- Distinguish **table stakes** (must-have to compete) from **differentiators** (must-have to win)
- Competitive analysis has a shelf life — note the date and flag areas that change fast
- Job postings are underrated intelligence — they signal strategic direction before announcements
- Customer reviews are gold — real users, unfiltered by marketing
- The most valuable part is the "so what" — don't skip strategic implications
- Pricing comparisons should note caveats (different packaging, usage-based vs seat-based, enterprise custom pricing)
- Be honest about competitor strengths — an analysis that always shows you winning is not credible
