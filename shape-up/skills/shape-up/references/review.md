# Shape Up: Review Mode

Produces structured, scored reviews of Shape Up pitches for self-review before sharing with a betting table. The goal is to catch everything — structural gaps, weak evidence, unresolved decisions, hidden scope, and missing rabbit holes — so the shaper can strengthen the pitch before it goes to a senior audience.

Read the full pitch before forming any assessment. Do not comment section by section as you read — synthesize first, then structure your output.

### Element-by-Element Feedback

For each element: one sentence on what's working, then named gaps and risks. Be specific — no generic Shape Up advice.

**Problem**
[What's working. Then: what's missing, what's asserted without evidence, what distinguishes symptoms from root cause, what the counterfactual is.]

**Appetite**
[What's working. Then: is this an estimate or a time box? Is it justified? Does the solution fit the appetite? What would be cut if the team were running over?]

**Solution**
[What's working. Then: is it over- or under-specified? What decisions are being deferred to the team that should be made now? Does the solution shape match the appetite? What's unclear?]

**Rabbit Holes**
[What's working. Then: which rabbit holes are named but unresolved? Which obvious risks are not named? Are any "rabbit holes" actually requirements in disguise?]

**Expected Revenue Impact**
[What's working. Then: is this a real estimate with visible math, or an assertion without reasoning? Is the mechanism clear — retained ARR, new ARR, or indirect enabler? Are any dollar figures placeholders ($X)? Does the impact claim match the scope of the solution?]

**No-Gos**
[What's working. Then: what's missing from the no-go list? Which no-gos are too vague to be enforceable? Does anything in the solution contradict a no-go?]

---

### Hard Blockers

List anything that must be resolved *before* the pitch goes to the betting table. Each blocker should be:
- Specific (not "strengthen the problem section")
- Owned (who needs to answer this?)
- Consequential (what goes wrong if it's unresolved?)

If there are no hard blockers, say so explicitly.

---

### Questions to Sit With

5–8 targeted questions the shaper should be able to answer before presenting. These are not rhetorical — they're genuine gaps where the answer would change how the pitch reads or whether the bet makes sense.

Format: question, then one sentence on why it matters.

---

### Holes to Watch

Risks or assumptions that don't rise to the level of blockers but could become problems during the cycle. Frame as "watch for X" — not as requirements or scope additions.

---

## Scoring Guide

Score each element 1–10. Anchor points:

**9–10** — Meets all Shape Up criteria, evidence-backed, no significant gaps
**7–8** — Strong with minor gaps; would pass a betting table with small additions
**5–6** — Adequate but soft; a betting table would have questions; needs work
**3–4** — Weak; key criteria missing or not credibly addressed
**1–2** — Element is present in name only or missing substantively

**Overall score** is not an average — weight Problem and Solution most heavily. A weak problem with a strong solution is still a weak pitch.

---

## Betting Table Readiness Guide

**READY** — All six elements are present and credible. No hard blockers. The betting table could make a decision with what's here.

**READY WITH CONDITIONS** — The pitch is shapeable but has named pre-conditions. List them explicitly. The pitch should not go to the betting table until they're resolved.

**NOT READY** — Fundamental elements are missing or the core bet is not yet established. Recommend what needs to happen first.

---

## Review Principles

**Be direct, not diplomatic.** The purpose of this review is to catch problems before a senior audience does. Softening feedback defeats the purpose.

**Distinguish what you know from what you infer.** If the pitch doesn't contain enough information to assess something, say so — don't assume the gap is filled somewhere else.

**Don't rewrite the pitch.** Surface gaps and ask questions. The shaper makes the decisions.

**Flag placeholder content as blockers.** $X amounts, [list here] prompts, and TBD sections are not stylistic — they're signals that the pitch isn't ready. Treat them as blockers unless the pitch explicitly acknowledges them and names a resolution path.

**Distinguish open questions from pre-conditions.** An open question is something the team can resolve during the cycle. A pre-condition is something that must be answered before the bet is made. Don't let the shaper treat pre-conditions as open questions.

**Shape Up is new at Bitly.** This means reviewers should be thorough, not lenient. Err on the side of asking one more question rather than assuming the shaper has the answer.
