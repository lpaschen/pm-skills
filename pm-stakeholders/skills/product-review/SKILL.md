# Product Review Skill

## Context

Bitly runs bi-weekly or monthly Product Reviews: short touchpoints between planning cycles where PMs bring work-in-progress to the CEO, CPO, and Sr Director of Product for **input**, not approval. The goal is to get strategic context and leadership signal early enough that it can actually change the outcome.

Kelsey (CPO) and Peter (CEO) are accountable to the board for outcomes. These sessions are how they stay close to the work between planning cycles — not to judge or approve, but to contribute.

---

## When to Use This Skill

- A PM is preparing a pre-read doc for an upcoming Product Review
- A PM wants to structure talking points or discussion questions for the session
- A PM needs to sharpen their recommendation before bringing it to leadership
- A PM wants to debrief after a review and capture decisions and follow-ups

---

## The Pre-Read Doc

The pre-read is shared with leadership **before** the session. It should be short enough to read in 5 minutes. The session is for discussion — not for presenting the doc.

### Pre-Read Template

```
## Product Review Pre-Read
**PM:** [Name]
**Date:** [Date]
**Session goal:** [What you need from this session — one sentence]

---

### What We're Working On
[2–3 sentences. What is the problem, who has it, and why does it matter now?]

### Where We Are
[Current state of the work — what's been validated, what's still open, what we've ruled out]

### Options Considered
| Option | Tradeoff |
|--------|----------|
| [Option A] | [What you gain / what you give up] |
| [Option B] | [What you gain / what you give up] |
| [Option C — if relevant] | [What you gain / what you give up] |

### Our Recommendation
[What you're leaning toward and the one-sentence rationale. Be direct — "We recommend X because Y."]

### What We Need From This Session
[Specific input you want. Be concrete: "Challenge our framing of the customer problem" or "React to the tradeoff between X and Y" — not "general feedback"]
```

---

## Running the Session

### Opening Ritual (30 seconds)
Start every session with a brief context set — do not skip this, even if the room knows the work:

> "We're here to get your input on [topic]. Last time we covered [X]. Today I want to focus on [specific question]. This is early-stage — we're looking for your reactions, not a decision."

This sets the right tone: collaborative, not ceremonial. The exec is a contributor, not a judge.

### Discussion Principles
- **Bring the work, not the deck.** A short doc beats slides for this format.
- **Name the real question.** If you're uncertain about the customer problem, say so. Don't package uncertainty as confidence.
- **Make the tradeoff visible.** Don't present a single path — show what you're giving up with each option.
- **Ask for what you need.** If you want to be challenged, say so. If you need a directional signal, say that.
- **Take notes in real time.** Capture what's said, not just what you agree with.

### What to Bring
Work that is **early enough that input can change the outcome**. Not:
- Finished decisions looking for a rubber stamp
- Shipping announcements
- Status updates (those belong in the weekly review)

Ideal topics:
- Problem framing that hasn't been validated yet
- A recommendation where you're genuinely uncertain
- A tradeoff with meaningful strategic implications
- Something where their board-level context or cross-team visibility would help

## After the Session

Capture within 24 hours:

```
## Product Review Debrief
**Date:** [Date]
**Topic:** [Topic]

### Key Input Received
- [Direct quote or paraphrase of the most important thing said]
- [Second signal]
- [Third signal]

### Decisions Made (if any)
- [Decision] — Owner: [Name]

### Open Questions
- [Question raised that wasn't resolved]

### What Changes as a Result
- [How this input changes the work, if at all]

### Follow-Ups
| Action | Owner | By When |
|--------|-------|---------|
| [Action] | [Name] | [Date] |
```

---

## Quality Checks

Before sending the pre-read, verify:
- [ ] The session goal is one clear sentence — not "get feedback"
- [ ] The recommendation is stated directly — not hedged into a list of options with no view
- [ ] "What we need from this session" is specific enough that leadership knows exactly how to be useful
- [ ] The work is genuinely in-progress — if the decision is already made, this is the wrong session
- [ ] The doc is readable in under 5 minutes

---

## Common Failure Modes

**Bringing finished work for approval.** This defeats the purpose. If it's done, ship it — don't review it.

**Asking for "general feedback."** Leadership can't help if they don't know what kind of help you need. Name the specific question.

**Over-presenting, under-discussing.** The session is 30–45 minutes. If you spend 20 of it presenting, you've wasted the room. Send the pre-read and trust that they read it.

**Burying the recommendation.** Don't make executives hunt for your view. State it clearly, then defend it.

**Treating the exec as a judge.** The tone is collaborative. You're not pitching — you're thinking together.
