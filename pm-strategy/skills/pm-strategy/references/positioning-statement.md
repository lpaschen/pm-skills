# Positioning Statement

This guide produces a positioning statement — a clear claim about who a product is for, what category it competes in, and why it wins against what the customer would otherwise use.

**Core concept:** Positioning is a claim about a gap in the market relative to the real alternative a customer would choose instead, which is often not the obvious competitor. The alternative can be a competing product, a manual workaround, or doing nothing.

**Format:** "For [target customer] who [need or opportunity], [product] is a [category] that [key benefit]. Unlike [primary alternative], [product] [key differentiator]."

**Inputs required before drafting:** A defined target segment (from persona work), the job the segment is hiring a solution for (from JTBD), and an honest list of what that segment currently uses instead, ranked by how likely they are to actually be losing to it.

**Common pitfalls:** Positioning for everyone, which position for no one. Leading with features instead of the benefit a customer gets. Naming a competitor as the "alternative" that isn't actually who you're losing deals to. A differentiator that isn't provable or is easily copied — if a competitor could paste the same sentence over their own product, it isn't a differentiator yet.

**Deliverable:** One positioning statement per segment (not one for the whole product, if segments differ), plus the ranked list of real alternatives it was built against so the statement can be re-tested as the market shifts.
